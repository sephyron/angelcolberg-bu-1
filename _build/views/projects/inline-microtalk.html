Next Generation Intranet

MicroTalk and the Next Generation Intranet at Microsoft

Volunteer project started in The Garage as an internal microblogging tool. Gained immediate adoption enabling it to win the Science Fair at Microsoft that year. Lots of rich user data around usage, needs and frequency enabled us to move forward as an Office Labs incubation project that would ultimately work its way across the enterprise and into our products and ultimately lead to the acquisition of Yammer.

The Next Generation Intranet Project (NGI) was created in order to bring all of our productivity applications together as the fist milestone in achieving the long term goal of a One Microsoft experience. This was a massive 2 year project that affected all of the major internal portals and information systems in place as we moved towards a more user-centric information model in order to address company wide issues around navigation, findability, document governance, messaging, tagging, ratings, web standards, accessibility, recommendation engines, taxonomy and branding.

Use Case

Overview

From experiment to collaboration platform

MicroTalk explored how an internal microblogging tool could support lightweight sharing, discovery, and conversation at enterprise scale. Usage data and feedback informed the product direction and its path toward broader collaboration tools.

Enterprise home and laptop workspace

Bring the experience into the enterprise

The laptop view shows how the MicroTalk interaction model was positioned within a broader intranet, connecting lightweight conversation with the enterprise information architecture.

Result

The concept made social activity feel native to the work environment instead of a disconnected experiment.

Conversation feed

Make updates easy to scan

The feed organizes short posts, authors, and activity into a stream that supports quick reading and lightweight participation.

Result

Users could understand what changed without opening every item.

Content discovery

Connect people and information

This view explores how navigation and content signals help employees find relevant conversations, documents, and activity across the intranet.

Result

Discovery was treated as a first-class task rather than an afterthought to publishing.

Enterprise navigation

Reduce portal fragmentation

The navigation concept brings major information areas into a common structure so users can predict where work and conversations belong.

Result

A shared navigation model reduced the cost of moving between internal properties.

Information model

Give content a durable home

The information model explores the relationship between spaces, documents, people, and activity that supports long-term enterprise findability.

Result

Content could be organized around user intent instead of the ownership of individual portals.

Profile and people context

Make collaboration personal

People and profile signals help users understand who is speaking, what expertise is nearby, and how a conversation connects to a team.

Result

Identity became useful context for finding and trusting enterprise information.

Messaging and tagging

Support lightweight contribution

The messaging and tagging patterns make it quick to post, classify, and retrieve updates without requiring a formal publishing workflow.

Result

Contribution stayed lightweight while the resulting content remained findable.

Document and workspace context

Join conversation to the work

This screen shows how documents and activity can share a workspace, allowing discussion to stay attached to the material it describes.

Result

Users could move from a conversation to the underlying work with less context switching.

Recommendations

Surface useful next steps

Recommendation patterns help the intranet suggest people, content, and activity based on the user’s current context.

Result

Discovery became more proactive without taking control away from the user.

Search and findability

Answer the findability problem

The search concept brings people, content, and conversations into one retrieval model, supporting both known-item search and exploration.

Result

Employees could search across the enterprise without knowing which portal owned the answer.

Activity and notifications

Keep participation visible

Activity signals give users a concise view of replies, mentions, and changes that require attention across their work.

Result

Notifications supported return visits without overwhelming the primary workspace.

Cross-portal governance

Scale a consistent model

The final concept addresses governance, standards, and shared patterns across the internal properties affected by the NGI program.

Result

A common foundation allowed individual portals to evolve without recreating the underlying experience.
