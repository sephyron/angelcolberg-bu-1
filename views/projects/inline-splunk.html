Splunk Observability Suite

I led product design work across Splunk's observability suite, with a focus on helping teams understand what real users experienced in an application. I worked across RUM, synthetic monitoring, service relationships, and logs so an investigation could move from a symptom to a likely cause. The design challenge was not exposing more telemetry; it was giving the telemetry a useful order and keeping the user journey visible while engineers investigated.

Real User Monitoring

Problem Definition

Teams needed to understand how real customers experienced their applications across pages, browsers, regions, and services. Performance signals were spread across dashboards, making it difficult to connect a slow page load to the affected user path or underlying service.

Hypothesis

A unified RUM workspace that combines page-load health, session context, service relationships, and regional signals will help teams move from an alert to a likely cause without switching between disconnected tools.

Method

Job stories, cognitive walkthroughs, and task-based evaluations were used with dashboard prototypes. The work focused on common investigation tasks: finding the worst-performing pages, tracing a user path, comparing regions, and identifying the service contributing to a slowdown.

Result and next steps

The direction centered the experience on real user impact rather than isolated metrics. A contextual map, clear severity hierarchy, and linked page, session, and service views created a stronger foundation for diagnosing issues and prioritizing the next investigation.

Iteration 1

Dark themed

Performance and observability teams worked in environments where dense data, long sessions, and frequent investigations made a dark workspace a practical fit. The visual system needed to preserve hierarchy across charts, maps, alerts, and diagnostic details without competing with the data.

The dark canvas also created a consistent foundation for the RUM, geo, path, and session views shown in the surrounding concepts.

Hypothesis

Method

Result and next steps

User Path Analysis

User Path Analysis

Sankey chart for real user path analysis

Hypothesis

Showing the sequence of pages and services as a connected flow will help investigators identify where users abandon a journey, where latency accumulates, and which paths deserve deeper analysis.

Methods

Sankey-style flow maps, task walkthroughs, and cognitive evaluations were used to test whether users could follow a path from entry point to drop-off and connect that path to the supporting page-load and service data.

Results and next steps

Task success and time to completion improved.

Hypothesis

Separating physical topologies and logical flow maps will allow our users' mental models to better match their roles, task divisions, and their usage frequency by having all the operations contextual.

Methods

Results
