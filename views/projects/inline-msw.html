MSW Redesign

Redesign of Microsoft's largest intranet site

I led the redesign of MSW, Microsoft's largest internal portal, using the MicroTalk information model and the emerging Metro visual language. The challenge was to make a very large site easier to navigate without flattening the needs of individual departments. I worked through navigation, content hierarchy, search, and team-space patterns so local information could live inside a consistent system.

Use Case

Findability across the enterprise

Unify the intranet foundation

The overview establishes the shared content, navigation, and Metro visual language that made Microsoft’s internal properties feel like one system.

Result

Employees received a clearer, more coherent starting point for finding information.

MSW home and navigation

Make the portal feel navigable

This screen combines the home surface with primary navigation and content signals, giving users a predictable way into a large internal site.

Result

Navigation and branding worked together instead of competing for attention.

Content landing page

Organize content around tasks

The landing layout groups related information into recognizable paths so users can scan for the right destination before opening a document or tool.

Result

Content hierarchy replaced the previous collection of disconnected links.

Department and team space

Support local context within a system

The team view gives a department room for its own content while retaining the shared global structure, navigation, and visual rules.

Result

Local ownership no longer required a separate portal pattern.

Search and discovery

Reduce the cost of finding information

The search experience gives employees a direct path to content while preserving the surrounding information architecture and portal context.

Result

Search became a connected part of the intranet rather than an isolated utility.

Content detail

Preserve context at the destination

The detail state keeps title, related information, and navigation available while users read or act on a piece of enterprise content.

Result

Users could complete tasks without losing their place in the broader portal.

Cross-property consistency

Turn branding into a usable system

The final state shows how Metro styling, content patterns, and navigation rules could travel across internal properties.

Result

The redesign created a reusable foundation instead of a one-off visual refresh.
