Observability and Distributed Tracing

As Principal Product Designer, I worked on Lightstep's observability and tracing tools. I focused on the moment when an engineer sees a latency or error spike and needs to find the service and span responsible across a distributed system. I designed a walkthrough for the correlations feature, refined the service-map investigation flow, and iterated on search so engineers could get to the right trace without memorizing query syntax.

Use Case

Trace Analysis and Service Diagram

Problem Definition

When latency or errors spiked, engineers had to manually correlate a histogram of span durations with a separate list of services to find which one was actually responsible, often across dozens of interdependent services.

Hypothesis

Pairing a latency histogram with percentile comparison against a live service topology diagram, and surfacing correlated tags automatically, would cut the time to isolate a root cause from minutes to seconds.

Method

Service maps and code prototypes were used to test the diagram interaction model: highlighting nodes with errors or added latency, and exposing per-service detail (instance count, platform, status) directly on the graph.

Result and next steps

The combined trace analysis table, correlations panel, and service diagram gave engineers a single workspace to go from "something is slow" to a specific service and trace, without switching tools mid-investigation.

Correlations Walkthrough and Search Improvements

Problem Definition

The correlations panel could point to a likely root cause, but new users did not understand how to read it or fold its findings into their investigation. Separately, the search bar required knowing the exact tag syntax, which slowed down anyone unfamiliar with the query language.

Hypothesis

An in-product walkthrough of the correlations feature would teach the root cause analysis flow in context, and a more intelligent search bar would let engineers filter traces without memorizing exact syntax.

Method

Designed a step-by-step walkthrough that introduces the correlations panel directly inside the Explorer workspace, and iterated on search bar behavior to support more flexible, forgiving query input.

Result and next steps

The walkthrough gave new users a clear path from "here is a correlation" to "here is why it matters," and the smarter search bar reduced the friction of getting to the right trace during an investigation.
